# Reconciliation: Tica Thesis (`regcm5-optimization.pdf`) vs. PRD + Addendum

**Method:** Extracted full text of the 71-page thesis PDF (`_bmad-source/regcm5-optimization.pdf`) via PyPDF2 and read it in full (Abstract, Ch. 1 Background, Ch. 2 Baseline Benchmarking/Profiling, Ch. 3 GPU porting of `getcape`, Ch. 4 Performance Portability, Ch. 5 Verification/Validation/Reproducibility, Ch. 6 Conclusions/Recommendations/Future Outlook, Appendices A/B). Cross-checked every item against `prd.md` (625 lines) and `addendum.md` (83 lines) via full read plus targeted `grep` for platform names, hardware terms, and memory-model terms.

## Summary Verdict

The PRD/addendum faithfully and extensively captured the thesis's **profiling methodology, the four confirmed hotspots and their speedups (1.77×/4.96×/7.10×/14.17×), the statistical-reproducibility numbers (tas/ps/huss MAD/RMSE), and the parallel-I/O-slowdown puzzle** — these are covered in real depth (FR-1–FR-4, FR-24–FR-27, FR-34, FR-35–FR-37, and corresponding addendum sections). However, **Chapter 4 (Performance Portability) was not captured at all**, and two of the thesis's four future-work items are absent from the PRD's FRs and Risk Register.

## Gaps Found

### 1. Performance-portability chapter (Ch. 4) and the superlinear-scaling anomaly — missing entirely
`grep` for "superlinear", "MareNostrum", "ORFEO", "H100", "performance portability" across both files returns zero hits. The thesis's Chapter 4 is a full chapter (not a footnote): it benchmarks the coupled RegCM5–CLM4.5 configuration on three platforms — Leonardo Booster (A100), MareNostrum 5 ACC (H100), and ORFEO (H100) — with a strong-scaling plot (Fig. 4.2) and node-level hardware comparison table (Table 4.1: CPU model, cores/node, NUMA domains, GPU model/memory/interconnect, network generation, per platform). The headline anomaly — "apparent superlinear scaling on MareNostrum 5 and Leonardo up to 16 GPUs, with efficiencies exceeding 100% relative to the 4-GPU baseline," explicitly named by the thesis as needing future investigation ("likely due to improved decomposition and memory-system effects rather than a true change in algorithmic complexity") — appears in both the Abstract and Conclusions chapter as one of the thesis's headline unresolved findings. This has no corresponding FR and no Risk Register line. Given the PRD's own stated methodology ("evidence, not intuition, behind every performance claim"), an unexplained cross-platform scaling anomaly in the thesis's own data is a natural candidate for a scoped investigation FR (parallel to how FR-34 was built from the I/O-slowdown puzzle) — as written, this program would neither know about nor budget for it.

### 2. CUDA managed memory vs. unified virtual memory (UVM) — the thesis's other flagged future-work item — missing entirely
`grep` for "managed memory", "unified memory", "mem:managed", "mem:unified", "UVM", "GH200" across both files returns zero hits. The thesis's Conclusions chapter states explicitly: "All the numerical experiments reported in the present work made use of the CUDA managed memory option for the memory model... Future investigations can also focus on the performance benefits of using the more advanced implementation of the CUDA unified virtual memory which is natively supported on the latest generation of NVIDIA devices and platforms such as the H100 and the GH200." This is a specific, named future-work item tied directly to hardware the PRD already discusses in other contexts (H100 is one of the two portability-benchmark platforms per gap #1, and GH200 is the platform where NVIDIA's "most advanced implementation" of UVM hardware support lives per Ch. 1.4). It's also mechanically relevant to FR-9/FR-11 (both concern data-movement/memory-residency strategy for future GPU porting) and to the addendum's own "Technical Lessons" section, which already discusses the *hybrid do concurrent + OpenACC data-region* strategy as an alternative to relying on the compiler's implicit (CUDA-managed) memory model — but never names CUDA managed memory as the mechanism currently in use, nor UVM as the next evolution to evaluate. This is a load-bearing omission: it's literally one of the thesis's four named future-work directions, and the PRD's I/O and reproducibility gaps (analogous future-work items) both got dedicated FRs, while this one got none.

### 3. Premise correction: the thesis does not have "six numbered future work items"
Chapter 6 ("Conclusions, Recommendations and Future Outlook") is prose, not an enumerated list. It names exactly **four** distinct future-work directions: (a) investigate the origin of the superlinear-scaling anomaly across platforms [gap #1 above — not covered]; (b) mitigate/reduce numerical divergence toward bitwise reproducibility [well covered — FR-36/FR-37]; (c) evaluate CUDA UVM (`mem:unified`) vs. the managed-memory model currently used, especially on H100/GH200 [gap #2 above — not covered]; (d) understand why parallel-write I/O modes underperform serial write [well covered — FR-34]. So 2 of the 4 actual future-work items made it into FRs, and 2 did not. Flagging this because the input brief's premise ("six numbered future work items") doesn't match the source document — worth correcting before this figure propagates further in the workflow.

### 4. FR-8's cross-vendor framing is vaguer than, and in one respect inconsistent with, the thesis's own compiler-support table
Thesis Table 1.2 (Ch. 1.4) gives specific, asymmetric CPU/GPU support per vendor for `do concurrent`: NVIDIA nvfortran — full CPU+GPU (OpenACC backend, since 2020); Intel ifx — full CPU+GPU (OpenMP backend, since 2022); HPE Cray — full CPU+GPU, both AMD and NVIDIA GPUs; GNU gfortran — **CPU-partial (auto-parallelization only), no GPU offload at all**; LLVM Flang — emerging (CPU)/pending (GPU). FR-8 as written asks for "verification exists that it parallelizes (not merely compiles) under GNU, Intel, and NVHPC" for every `do concurrent` loop, and the Risk Register entry describes the risk generically as "parallelizes unevenly across compiler vendors — a loop can silently run serial, not fail to build." Per the thesis's own table, this is imprecise for GNU specifically: GNU gfortran has **no GPU offload path for `do concurrent` at all**, so "verify it parallelizes under GNU" can only ever mean CPU-side auto-parallelization — there is no GPU parallelization outcome to check for GNU, silent-serial or otherwise. FR-8 doesn't distinguish this from the Intel/NVHPC cases, where GPU parallelization is a real, checkable outcome that can silently degrade to serial execution. A reader implementing FR-8's checklist would reasonably expect a uniform "did it parallelize on GPU y/n" check across all three vendors, which the thesis's table says is a category error for GNU. Separately, HPE Cray — which the thesis's table shows has the broadest support (both AMD and NVIDIA GPUs) — is not mentioned anywhere in the PRD despite the project's own "AMD where feasible" target (§5 Non-Goals, Open Question 4); Cray would be the relevant compiler for a future AMD `do concurrent` GPU path, and the thesis flags it as already mature. This is worth a small correction to FR-8's wording (e.g., "verify CPU-parallelization under GNU; verify GPU-parallelization under Intel and NVHPC; Cray noted as a future path pending AMD GPU access") rather than the current one-size-fits-three-vendors phrasing.

## Items Checked and Found Adequately Covered (no gap)

- **Hardware/software environment (Table 2.2):** NVHPC v24.5, HPC-X OpenMPI v2.19, CUDA Toolkit v12.4, PnetCDF v1.12.3 are all named explicitly in the PRD's "Version-alignment note" (§ Technology and Dependency Targets), correctly framed as one-to-two versions behind the program's stated targets (NVHPC 26.3, HPC-X 2.25, CUDA 12.9). FR-35 explicitly scopes re-validation under current versions rather than assuming carryover.
- **Reproducibility / statistical-divergence numbers:** tas/ps/huss MAD/RMSE/rMAD/rRMSE figures are captured with reasonable fidelity in the addendum's "Reproducibility Gap" section and used correctly as FR-36/FR-37's targets, including the correct causal attribution (moisture-field sensitivity for huss) and correct scoping caveat (bitwise reproducibility was explicitly out of the thesis's scope).
- **Parallel-I/O slowdown puzzle:** the exact numbers (sequential 3.7h vs. HDF5-parallel 7.7h vs. PnetCDF-parallel 4.0h at 800 cores) and the "thesis names this as open future work" framing are both captured accurately in FR-34 and the addendum.
- **Four confirmed hotspots and porting strategies:** getcape/CAPE-CIN (54%), CLM-hydrology memset (~20%), interp1d_r8 (~12%), heatindex (~8%), the three offloading strategies tried (OpenACC / do concurrent / hybrid) and their comparable performance, and the concrete porting hazards (automatic-array allocation, helper-procedure scoping, the `public`-declaration quirk, implicit-copy inefficiency) are all captured in Feature 4.1/4.3 and the addendum's "Technical Lessons" section, in more mechanism-level detail than the thesis itself states in some cases (correctly attributed).
- **Reproducibility tooling (Appendix B):** `concat_diff_var.py`, `multi_day_stats_generic.py`, `spatial_maps_generic.py` are correctly identified and adopted as the basis for FR-24's regression-diff tool rather than building a new one.

## Recommendation

Before this PRD is finalized, consider adding:
1. A scoped investigation FR (or an extension of FR-9/FR-11's remit) for the cross-platform superlinear-scaling anomaly, with a Risk Register line analogous to the existing I/O-slowdown entry.
2. A scoped FR (or explicit note under FR-11/FR-22) evaluating CUDA UVM (`mem:unified`) against the currently-used managed-memory model (`mem:managed`), scoped to H100/GH200-class hardware where the thesis says UVM hardware support is most mature — this could plausibly piggyback on FR-11's halo-exchange/GPUDirect investigation since both concern the data-movement model, or stand alone.
3. A wording fix to FR-8 (and the Risk Register line citing it) to reflect the thesis's Table 1.2 asymmetry: GNU has no `do concurrent` GPU path to verify at all (CPU-only auto-parallelization check), versus Intel/NVHPC where GPU-parallelization-vs-silent-serial is the real risk being checked.
4. Correct the internal assumption (if it exists elsewhere in the BMad workflow state) that the thesis enumerates "six" future-work items — it names four, in prose, in Chapter 6.
