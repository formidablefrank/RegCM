## Technical Lessons from the Source Thesis: `do concurrent`/OpenACC Porting Hazards

The thesis's GPU-porting of `getcape`, `interp1d_r8`, and `heatindex` surfaced concrete, hard-won constraints worth generalizing to any future porting FR under this program (FR-9, FR-10, FR-11, FR-35):

- **No automatic-array allocation inside an offloaded procedure.** `getcape`'s host version allocates automatic arrays internally; when offloaded as a device kernel, this is unsupported. The fix pattern: allocate the needed work arrays *outside* the procedure and pass them in as arguments. Any future porting FR touching a procedure with internal automatic allocation needs this same refactor first.
- **Helper procedures must be raised to module scope and marked `pure` + `!$acc routine() seq`.** Fortran's `do concurrent` standard only permits calling `pure` procedures inside the construct; contained (nested) helper functions must be pulled out to module level, and both the main procedure and its helpers need the OpenACC `routine` directive so the compiler generates device-callable versions.
- **Correction (per `project-context.md`, which supersedes the thesis narrative on this point): the `public`-declaration constraint does not apply to the pattern actually merged into the codebase.** The thesis explored three strategies for `getcape` (pure OpenACC, pure `do concurrent`, and a hybrid); it was in the `do concurrent` variant specifically that removing `public` was needed. The pattern actually merged uses **OpenACC** (`!$acc parallel loop` at the call site, `!$acc routine seq` on the called procedure, `pure`), and under that pattern `getcape_new` remains `public` and offloads successfully — confirmed directly in the current codebase. Do not generalize "remove `public`" as a checklist item for future OpenACC porting FRs; it is specific to the `do concurrent` path this project did not merge.
- **The compiler's implicit data-movement inference is inconsistent and sometimes suboptimal.** For the `do concurrent`-only offload of `getcape`, NVHPC sometimes emitted an implicit `copy` (both directions) for 2D/3D work arrays where the more efficient `copyout`-only would suffice; the thesis's fix was wrapping the `do concurrent` region in an explicit OpenACC `!$acc data create(...)` region to take that decision away from the compiler. This is directly relevant to FR-9/FR-11: relying on `do concurrent`'s implicit CUDA-managed-memory model alone may not be enough for the highest-value ports; explicit OpenACC data-region control alongside `do concurrent` (the thesis's "hybrid" strategy) performed comparably to either pure approach and gives more predictable data movement.
- **All three strategies tried (pure OpenACC, pure `do concurrent`, hybrid `do concurrent` + OpenACC data regions) delivered comparable speedups** for `getcape` in the thesis's own benchmarking. **The pattern actually merged into the codebase is pure OpenACC** (per `project-context.md`), not `do concurrent` or the hybrid — so FR-8's cross-vendor `do concurrent` parallelization check does not directly apply to these four hotspots; their portability concern is OpenACC's own uneven vendor support (strongest on NVIDIA, per the Technology Stack Analysis in the research note), a separate axis from `do concurrent`'s. FR-9's feasibility work on RRTMG or other new candidates should not assume one strategy is categorically superior from this precedent alone — the choice may come down to which is easier to maintain and verify per subsystem, not raw performance, but the project's own working example (`getcape_new`) is OpenACC, not `do concurrent`.

## The Parallel-I/O-Slowdown Puzzle (FR-34) — What Is Already Known

The thesis's own measurement (§2.3 of the source PDF) tested three I/O configurations for diagnostic output in the CLM4.5-coupled configuration: HDF5-parallel write, HDF5-sequential write, and PnetCDF-parallel write, with parallel reads in all cases. At every tested rank count (400/800 CPUs; 8/16/32/64 GPUs), sequential write was fastest and PnetCDF-parallel was frequently the slowest of the three — e.g., at 800 CPU cores: sequential 3.7 h, HDF5-parallel 7.7 h, PnetCDF-parallel 4.0 h. The thesis explicitly did not investigate why, naming it as future work. FR-34 inherits this as a concrete, bounded starting question rather than an open-ended "profile everything" mandate — the first thing to explain is why *enabling* parallelism on the write path makes it slower, before any recommendation about defaults is made.

**Related correction, same theme**: the original PRD draft, following the technical research note's inference from output-write cadence (3–6 simulated hours) alone, framed diagnostic I/O generally as a burst-cost problem at high rank counts rather than a sustained one. regcm5-dev corrected this during review: I/O measurably dominates wall-clock time in practice at rank counts higher than the `Testing/` fixtures typically exercise. Recorded here because it is a case where direct operational experience overrode a reasonable-looking structural inference — a reminder that the research note's hotspot rankings (explicitly caveated there as inference, not measurement) should yield to real profiling data (FR-4) or operator experience wherever the two conflict.

## The Reproducibility Gap — What "Statistical Reproducibility" Currently Means

The thesis's own reproducibility assessment (§5 of the source PDF) explicitly states bitwise reproducibility was *not* pursued; only statistical divergence was measured, using MAD/RMSE/rMAD/rRMSE (defined in the Glossary) computed via a documented Python/NCO workflow (`concat_diff_var.py`, `multi_day_stats_generic.py`, `spatial_maps_generic.py`, Appendix B of the source PDF). After 7 model days: `tas` (near-surface air temperature) rMAD/rRMSE stayed under 0.06%/0.17%; `ps` (surface pressure) under 0.008%/0.019%; `huss` (near-surface specific humidity) rose to roughly 2%/4% (with hourly peaks reported up to ~10% in localized regions). The thesis attributes `huss`'s larger divergence to moisture fields' greater sensitivity to small perturbations in transport and boundary-layer exchange.

**Resolution (Open Question 9, final)**: this went through two rounds. regcm5-dev first answered that bit-exactness remains required, which would have made the four already-merged GPU ports non-compliant. Input reconciliation against `project-context.md` then surfaced a direct conflict: that document states, as an established project precedent, that CPU-to-GPU porting specifically is accepted at statistical (not bitwise) reproducibility, citing these same thesis bounds as the reference. Raised back to regcm5-dev, the documented precedent was confirmed as governing. Net result: the project runs a **two-tier numerical policy** — CPU-only changes default to bit-exact (unchanged), while CPU-to-GPU porting specifically is accepted at the thesis's measured statistical bounds. The four already-merged ports were never non-compliant; FR-36 (which assumed otherwise) is retired, and FR-25/FR-37 encode the two-tier policy directly. Likely sources of the (accepted) divergence, per the thesis's own discussion of HPC reproducibility challenges (§5 intro) and useful background for anyone later asked to tighten these bounds: floating-point non-associativity from reduction/summation reordering under parallel execution, fused-multiply-add (FMA) contraction differences between CPU and GPU code paths, and out-of-order execution differences under OpenACC scheduling.

## Options Considered: BMI Resolution (FR-17)

| Option | Argument for | Argument against |
|---|---|---|
| **Implement to CSDMS BMI 2.0 spec** | Enables genuine external coupling via a recognized standard; unblocks any future consumer without a bespoke interface. | The guarded-out code is leftover tutorial boilerplate referencing fields RegCM5's `atm_model` type doesn't have — this is closer to a fresh implementation than a repair. Real effort, with no known consumer today. |
| **Formally deprecate and remove** | Removes a misleading, non-functional artifact; honest about current state; zero effort beyond documentation and code removal. | Forecloses BMI-based coupling without a deliberate decision that it's unwanted — if a consumer materializes later, someone starts from zero rather than from a partial (if broken) attempt. |

Recommendation left open in the PRD (Open Question 1) because it hinges on a fact only regcm5-dev can supply: whether any concrete external consumer is expected. If none is anticipated within a reasonable horizon, deprecation is the lower-risk default — a broken, dormant "contract" is worse than an honest absence of one, since it invites a future contributor to assume it works.

## Options Considered: GPU-Porting Strategy for RRTMG (FR-9)

| Option | Precedent | Risk profile |
|---|---|---|
| **Directive/`do concurrent` port of existing RRTMG kernels** | ICON-A attempted this for PSrad (RRTMG's own lineage) and did not reach acceptable speedup. | High risk of repeating a documented failure; low implementation cost if it fails fast. |
| **Scheme replacement (RTE+RRTMGP-style)** | ICON-A's resolution after the directive-port failure — RTE+RRTMGP was designed for GPU from the start, including separate CPU/GPU code paths. | High implementation cost (effectively a new radiation scheme integration); would be a numerics-review-level change, not a mechanical port; explicitly out of scope for this PRD (§5) even if FR-9 recommends it. |
| **No GPU porting of radiation** | Radiation is already called far less frequently than dynamics (`dtrad` = 18× `dt`); the cost may be acceptable to leave on CPU while the rest of the model runs on GPU. | Leaves a potential CPU/GPU synchronization cost at each radiation call if the rest of the timestep is GPU-resident; needs FR-4 data to evaluate. |

FR-9 is scoped as an investigation specifically so this three-way choice is made with FR-4's measured data in hand, not before it exists.

## Technical Evidence Detail: Why the Instrumentation Gap Matters

From the research note: `time_begin`/`time_end` (DEBUG-gated, `Main/mpplib/mod_service.F90`) instrumentation exists at ~130 call sites across 38 files, covering BATS/surface, tendency/advection, diffusion, all cumulus schemes except shallow convection, some chemistry support routines, and the outer radiation driver (`mod_rad_colmod3.F90`). It is absent from RRTMG's actual kernels (`mod_rrtmg_driver.F90`, `rrtmg_lw_rad.F90`, `rrtmg_sw_rad.F90`), the KPP integrators themselves (`mod_cb6_Integrator.F90`, `mod_cbmz_integrator.F90`), `mod_ncout.F90`, and WSM5/WSM7/WDM7 microphysics. FR-1–FR-3 target exactly the three subsystems in that gap that the code-size and call-frequency analysis in the research note flags as most likely to matter.

## Technical Evidence Detail: `assignpnt` Pointer-Aliasing Mechanism

Subsystems exchange data via `Share/mod_memutil.F90`'s `assignpnt` (a ~45-overload generic pointer-remap interface), aliasing each subsystem's small private exchange-type fields onto shared module-level derived-type state (`slice`, `surfstate`, `atmstate_tendency` in `Main/mpplib/mod_regcm_types.F90`) once at scheme initialization. Example (`mod_pbl_interface.F90:94-186`): PBL's own output fields (`p2m%tten`, `p2m%uten`) are pointer-aliased onto the shared atmosphere-tendency array (`aten%t`, `aten%u`) that the dynamical core later consumes directly — no copy, no message-passing step. This is why GPU data-residency cannot be inferred automatically by the compiler for any subsystem reached through this mechanism: the aliasing relationship is established at runtime, not visible in a given subroutine's own signature.

**OASIS3-MCT** (`namelist /oasisparam/`): imports `l_cpl_im_sst/wz0/wust` (SST, roughness, friction velocity); exports `l_cpl_ex_u10m/v10m/wspd/wdir/t2m/q2m/slp/taux/tauy/z0/ustr/evap/prec/nuwa/ulhf/ushf/uwlw/dwlw/nulw/uwsw/dwsw/ndsw/rhoa`. Under `#ifdef ECLM`, additional atmospheric T/U/V/Q, geopotential height, runoff, snow, soil moisture/temperature, albedo, CH4 flux, dust/VOC flux, dry-deposition velocity.

**REGESM** (`mod_update.F90`, `imp_data`/`exp_data` types): imports `sst`, `sit` (sea-ice thickness), `msk`, `zo`, `ustar`; exports `psfc, tsfc, qsfc, swrd, lwrd, dlwr, lhfx, shfx, prec, evap, wndu, wndv, rnof, taux, tauy, wspd, wdir, ustr, nflx, sflx, snow, dswr, rhoa`.

These are independent field lists on independent code paths — FR-13/FR-14 should not attempt to unify them into a shared abstraction as part of "documentation," since that would be a design change beyond this PRD's scope.

## Multi-Platform Performance Portability (thesis Chapter 4 — not scoped into this program)

The source thesis also benchmarked the GPU-enabled CLM4.5-coupled configuration across three HPC platforms — Leonardo Booster plus two others, MareNostrum 5 ACC and ORFEO H100 — finding apparent superlinear strong-scaling efficiency (>100% relative to a 4-GPU baseline) on MareNostrum 5 and Leonardo between 4 and 16 GPUs, with the thesis explicitly flagging the cause as unexplained future work. This program does not scope any FR to investigate that anomaly or to validate performance portability beyond Leonardo — see Open Question 10 for whether that should change.

## Notes on Phasing Rationale

Phase 1 items were selected for the combination of low implementation risk and high leverage over later decisions — FR-1–FR-4 unlock evidence-based scoping for Phases 2–3; FR-18, FR-20, FR-21, FR-23 are small, self-contained corrections with no numerical risk. Phase 3 is ordered last specifically because FR-9 (the highest-uncertainty item in the whole program) benefits from FR-4's baseline existing first — sequencing GPU-porting investigation before profiling would repeat the inference-based decision-making this program is meant to replace.

FR-24–FR-27 (Automated Regression Infrastructure) is the exception to Phase 1's "small and cheap" character: it is placed there anyway because every other feature's acceptance discipline (Build/Test/Numerical/Performance) depends on it, and doing it early means every later phase gets to use it rather than continuing the manual NCO/CDO workflow throughout the whole program. It should be estimated and resourced as its own real task, not folded into the same rough sizing as its Phase 1 neighbors — this matches the governing project context's own explicit warning against treating regression-tool work as a quick add-on.

**Revised phasing (post-review):** regcm5-dev reordered the original 3-phase draft into 4 phases, with two notable consequences worth recording:

- **FR-31 (GitHub Actions CI pipeline) was briefly considered for Phase 1** (ahead of containers) but regcm5-dev settled on keeping it in Phase 3, co-landing with FR-28/FR-29/FR-30 (containers) and FR-32 (GPU-path CI coverage) — the whole Feature 4.9 pipeline now builds directly from the container images from day one, rather than starting native and switching later. Until Phase 3, Phases 1–2 rely on FR-24–FR-27's regression-diff tool used on-demand (manually invoked on the HPC cluster), not through a triggered pipeline — this is a real gap (no CI gating merges during Phases 1–2) but was a deliberate sequencing choice, not an oversight.
- **Coupling (Feature 4.4, all of FR-13–FR-17) moved to a new Phase 4**, sequenced after GPU acceleration and containerization rather than alongside I/O scaling. This is a priority choice, not a technical dependency — nothing in Phases 1–3 blocks or is blocked by coupling work. It does mean OASIS3-MCT/REGESM regression hardening (FR-13/FR-14) will, for a while, still rely on manual validation for coupled builds specifically, even after FR-24–FR-27's regression-diff tool exists for the non-coupled `Testing/` fixtures it's initially built against — worth revisiting whether the regression-diff tool should be extended to coupled-build fixtures when Phase 4 begins, rather than assumed to already cover them.
